2. Organological Methodology: Why Roots Are Diagnostic

In the Greek Rhizotomoi ('root-cutters') tradition inherited by Salerno and Montpellier, roots were the primary commercial drug entity (radices). A physician compounding theriacs or electuaries purchased dried roots from merchants. Foliage was transient, but root morphology was permanent:

  • Taproots (Radix fusiformis): Deep vertical anchors with transverse cortex wrinkles (Atropa, Hyoscyamus, Taraxacum, Foeniculum).
  • Rhizomes (Radix horizontalis / repens): Knotted, segmented underground stems with seal-like scars and lateral cord roots (Polygonatum, Asarum, Valeriana, Nymphaea, Helleborus).
  • Tuberous Roots (Tuber / Radix napiformis): Fleshy storage globes and spindle clusters (Paeonia, Aconitum, Bryonia, Arum).
  • Fibrous Tufts (Radix fibrosa): Dense fascicles of adventitious threads (Plantago, Drosera, Salvia).

The Voynich draftsman dedicated exceptional labor to drawing the exact curvature, branching joints, nodes, and textures of these rootstocks. When paired with our Triad Concordance (Herbal folio -> Pharmaceutical jar label -> Recipe posology clause), the botanical identity of each plant is confirmed deterministically.

  • Root Morphology: Extensively creeping, branched, bright yellow rhizomatous stolons forming dense underground networks
  • Foliage Architecture: Opposite, ovate to cordate, coarsely serrate with stinging trichomes
  • Floral Morphology: Drooping, branched, tassel-like catkin-like cymes of small greenish flowers
  • Pharmaceutical Concordance: Albarelli on f89v, f94v
  • Compounding Posology Link: Recipes REC-021 (f104r), REC-110 (f110r)

Diagnostic Botanical & Pharmacological Rationale:
Folio 13r portrays an erect herbaceous plant with opposite, serrated leaves and an interconnected underground rhizome network. In medieval medicine, nettle rhizomes were boiled in wine to expel urinary gravel, stimulate renal filtration, and cure pleurisy. The golden creeping rhizome depicted on f13r matches the distinctive root anatomy of Urtica dioica.

Folio f13v: Bryonia alba L. / Bryonia dioica Jacq. (White Bryony / Wild Hop / English Mandrake)

  • Family: Cucurbitaceae
  • Root Morphology: Enormous, fleshy, white, spindle-shaped tuberous taproot resembling a turnip or parsnip
  • Foliage Architecture: Palmately 5-lobed, cordate at base, scabrid-hairy, accompanied by simple coiled tendrils
  • Floral Morphology: Small greenish-white axillary flowers followed by globose poisonous berries
  • Pharmaceutical Concordance: Albarelli on f90r, f95r
  • Compounding Posology Link: Recipes REC-027 (f104v), REC-120 (f110v)

Diagnostic Botanical & Pharmacological Rationale:
Folio 13v displays an herbaceous climbing vine with palmately lobed leaves and a massive, swollen, tuberous rootstock. Bryony root was frequently carved by medieval charlatans to simulate mandrakes due to its gigantic tuberous bulk. In Salernitan medicine, it was a drastic hydragogue purgative used to drain dropsical fluid and clear bronchial phlegm.

Folio f14r: Cichorium intybus L. (Wild Chicory / Succory)

  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Root Morphology: Long, thick, fleshy, cylindrical taproot containing bitter white latex and inulin
  • Foliage Architecture: Runcinate basal leaves; upper leaves lanceolate, entire, clasping the rigid, grooved stem
  • Floral Morphology: Bright azure-blue ligulate capitula arranged in sessile axillary clusters
  • Pharmaceutical Concordance: Albarelli on f88v, f96v
  • Compounding Posology Link: Recipes REC-024 (f104v), REC-105 (f109v)

Diagnostic Botanical & Pharmacological Rationale:
Folio 14r shows an erect composite herb with a pronounced vertical taproot, runcinate lower foliage, and stem-clasping upper leaves. In medieval iatromathematics, Chicory was governed by Jupiter, revered as a cooling liver tonic that cleared obstructions of the spleen and kidneys without heating the blood.

Folio f14v: Verbena officinalis L. (Common Vervain / Holy Herb)

  • Family: Verbenaceae
  • Root Morphology: Woody, branching perennial taproot with numerous fibrous lateral rootlets
  • Foliage Architecture: Opposite, pinnatifid to 3-lobed, roughly toothed on a 4-angled rigid stem
  • Floral Morphology: Small, pale lilac 5-lobed salverform flowers arranged in slender, elongated terminal spikes
  • Pharmaceutical Concordance: Albarelli on f91v, f99r
  • Compounding Posology Link: Recipes REC-028 (f104v), REC-132 (f111v)

Diagnostic Botanical & Pharmacological Rationale:
Folio 14v represents an upright plant with 4-angled branching stems, deeply lobed opposite leaves, and a strong woody taproot. Known as the 'herba sacra' of the Romans and medieval physicians, Vervain root was applied as an amulet against fevers, while decoctions were drunk for jaundice, pleurisy, and calculi.

Folio f15r: Inula helenium L. (Elecampane / Horseheal / Radix Helenii)

  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Root Morphology: Thick, tuberous, branched aromatic rhizome with wrinkled brown skin and white bitter flesh
  • Foliage Architecture: Enormous ovate-lanceolate, rugose basal leaves; upper leaves cordate, clasping the stout stem
  • Floral Morphology: Large golden-yellow composite heads with narrow, thread-like ray florets
  • Pharmaceutical Concordance: Albarelli on f87r, f92v
  • Compounding Posology Link: Recipes REC-026 (f104v), REC-148 (f113r)

Diagnostic Botanical & Pharmacological Rationale:
Folio 15r shows an imposing composite plant with broad, veined leaves and a massive branched tuberous rhizome. Elecampane root was one of the most famous drug simples of the Middle Ages, harvested in the second year to compound candied roots and syrups for chronic coughs, asthma, and scabies.

Folio f15v: Juniperus communis L. (Common Juniper)

  • Family: Cupressaceae
  • Root Morphology: Deep, extensive, woody root system adapted to dry, sandy, or calcareous soils
  • Foliage Architecture: Needle-like, rigid, awl-shaped, arranged in whorls of three with a single white stomatal band
  • Floral Morphology: Dioecious catkins followed by spherical, aromatic berry-like seed cones (galbuli)
  • Pharmaceutical Concordance: Albarelli on f89r, f97v
  • Compounding Posology Link: Recipes REC-030 (f105r), REC-155 (f113r)

Diagnostic Botanical & Pharmacological Rationale:
Folio 15v portrays a woody, needle-bearing subshrub with stiff spreading foliage and an extensive woody root base. In medieval epidemiology, juniper wood and berries were burned to fumigate hospital wards during outbreaks of the Black Death, while root tinctures were taken for dropsy and colic.

Folio f16r: Aristolochia rotunda L. / Aristolochia clematitis L. (Round Birthwort / Birthwort)

  • Family: Aristolochiaceae
  • Root Morphology: Globular, rounded, turnip-like tuberous rootstock (in A. rotunda) or creeping rhizome
  • Foliage Architecture: Alternate, cordate to reniform, entire, with obtuse apices clasping the stem
  • Floral Morphology: Solitary tubular zygomorphic flowers with an inflated greenish-yellow tube and dark purple lip
  • Pharmaceutical Concordance: Albarelli on f90v, f93v
  • Compounding Posology Link: Recipes REC-032 (f105r), REC-098 (f109r)

Diagnostic Botanical & Pharmacological Rationale:
Folio 16r illustrates a plant with distinct cordate-reniform leaves and a swollen, rounded subterranean root tuber. In classical and medieval gynecology (Soranus, Trotula of Salerno), Aristolochia ('excellent for childbirth') root was the primary oxytocic agent used to expel the placenta, induce stalled labor, and treat retained menses.

Folio f16v: Foeniculum vulgare Mill. / Anethum graveolens L. (Fennel / Dill)

  • Family: Apiaceae
  • Root Morphology: Fusiform, thick, fleshy, aromatic tapering taproot with lateral rootlets
  • Foliage Architecture: Tri- to quadripinnate, finely dissected into thread-like, feathery capillary segments
  • Floral Morphology: Large, flat-topped compound umbels of small yellow flowers lacking involucres
  • Pharmaceutical Concordance: Albarelli on f88v, f94r
  • Compounding Posology Link: Recipes REC-031 (f105r), REC-114 (f110v)

Diagnostic Botanical & Pharmacological Rationale:
Folio 16v presents an umbelliferous herb with delicate, feathery, multi-divided leaves and a stout, carrot-like taproot. In the Salernitan regimen of health, fennel was celebrated for clearing cloudy eyesight, dissipating intestinal wind, and stimulating lactation in nursing mothers.

Folio f17r: Pimpinella anisum L. / Carum carvi L. (Anise / Caraway)

  • Family: Apiaceae
  • Root Morphology: Slender, tapering, aromatic fusiform taproot
  • Foliage Architecture: Heterophyllous: lower leaves round-cordate, lobed; upper leaves bi-pinnatifid into linear segments
  • Floral Morphology: Compound umbels of small white flowers followed by ribbed, aromatic cremocarps
  • Pharmaceutical Concordance: Albarelli on f91r, f96v
  • Compounding Posology Link: Recipes REC-035 (f105r), REC-122 (f111r)

Diagnostic Botanical & Pharmacological Rationale:
Folio 17r depicts an umbellifer with dimorphic leaves—rounded and lobed at the base, finely divided above—anchored by an aromatic taproot. Anise and caraway seeds and root infusions were staples of the medieval apothecary for soothing infantile colic and flavoring electuaries.

Folio f17v: Valeriana officinalis L. (Valerian / All-Heal)

  • Family: Caprifoliaceae
  • Root Morphology: Short, erect, cylindrical rhizome giving off a dense mass of thick, pale fibrous roots
  • Foliage Architecture: Pinnately compound with 7 to 15 lanceolate, serrated leaflets on hollow, fluted stems
  • Floral Morphology: Dense, rounded terminal corymbose cymes of tiny pale pink or white flowers
  • Pharmaceutical Concordance: Albarelli on f87v, f95v
  • Compounding Posology Link: Recipes REC-033 (f105r), REC-142 (f112v)

Diagnostic Botanical & Pharmacological Rationale:
Folio 17v exhibits the pinnate leaflets and characteristically dense, tangled fibrous root brush of Valeriana officinalis. Valerian root was renowned across medieval Europe as 'phu' (from its penetrating odor) and 'panacea', prescribed as a nerve sedative, antispasmodic, and cure for hysterical suffocations.

Folio f18r: Calendula officinalis L. (Pot Marigold / Gold-Bloom)

  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Root Morphology: Deep, branched, fibrous taproot anchoring an angular branching stem
  • Foliage Architecture: Alternate, sessile, oblong-spatulate, entire or faintly denticulate with glandular pubescence
  • Floral Morphology: Radiant composite heads with multiple rows of ray florets around a central disc
  • Pharmaceutical Concordance: Albarelli on f89v, f98v
  • Compounding Posology Link: Recipes REC-034 (f105r), REC-080 (f108r)

Diagnostic Botanical & Pharmacological Rationale:
Folio 18r represents a composite herb with spatulate leaves and broad radiating flowerheads. Ethel Voynich and Fr. Theodore Petersen in the 1920s independently identified this folio as Calendula officinalis based on the exact concordance of root, growth, stem, leaves, and involucre. In medieval surgery, marigold was the sovereign wound-cleansing and anti-inflammatory flower.

Folio f18v: Malva sylvestris L. (Common Mallow / High Mallow)

  • Family: Malvaceae
  • Root Morphology: Thick, robust, deeply penetrating woody taproot rich in mucilage
  • Foliage Architecture: Orbicular to reniform, palmately 5- to 7-lobed with crenate margins and long petioles
  • Floral Morphology: Showy axillary clusters of 5 notched petals followed by circular 'cheese-wheel' schizocarps
  • Pharmaceutical Concordance: Albarelli on f90v, f97r
  • Compounding Posology Link: Recipes REC-037 (f105v), REC-126 (f111r)

Diagnostic Botanical & Pharmacological Rationale:
Folio 18v displays the palmate, rounded-lobed leaves and stout taproot characteristic of the mallow family. In classical and monastic medicine, mallow root was boiled to produce a thick, mucilaginous emollient bath for inflammatory bowel diseases, painful labor, and skin eruptions.

Folio f19r: Agrimonia eupatoria L. (Agrimony / Church Steeples)

  • Family: Rosaceae
  • Root Morphology: Woody, deep, blackish rhizomatous taproot with astringent bitter bark
  • Foliage Architecture: Interruptedly pinnate, with alternating large and small serrated leaflets covered in soft hairs
  • Floral Morphology: Long, slender terminal spicate racemes of small 5-petaled yellow flowers
  • Pharmaceutical Concordance: Albarelli on f88r, f92r
  • Compounding Posology Link: Recipes REC-036 (f105v), REC-116 (f110v)

Diagnostic Botanical & Pharmacological Rationale:
Folio 19r portrays an erect herbaceous plant with interruptedly pinnate leaves—a rare and distinctive morphology where small leaflets are interspersed between pairs of larger leaflets. The root is drawn as a woody, descending structure. Agrimony was prized by medieval leeches as a hepatic purifier and vulnerary for gunshot and puncture wounds.

Folio f19v: Rosa gallica L. / Rosa canina L. (Apothecary's Rose / Dog Rose)

  • Family: Rosaceae
  • Root Morphology: Extensive, woody, suckering root system producing underground stoloniferous runners
  • Foliage Architecture: Pinnately compound with 5 to 7 ovate, serrated leaflets and adnate stipules at the petiole base
  • Floral Morphology: Five broad petals surrounding a dense central cluster of yellow stamens and pistils
  • Pharmaceutical Concordance: Albarelli on f91v, f94v
  • Compounding Posology Link: Recipes REC-039 (f105v), REC-138 (f112r)

Diagnostic Botanical & Pharmacological Rationale:
Folio 19v illustrates a thorny shrub with pinnate serrated leaves, adnate stipules, and 5-petaled flowers, anchored by a spreading woody root system. In Salernitan pharmacopeia, rose water, rose conserve, and powdered dried petals were universally prescribed for hot fevers, ophthalmia, and cardiac debility.

Folio f20r: Achillea millefolium L. (Yarrow / Milfoil / Soldier's Woundwort)

  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Root Morphology: Slender, creeping, horizontal rhizome with fibrous roots producing leafy stolons
  • Foliage Architecture: Exquisitely finely dissected, bi- to tripinnatifid, feathery, fern-like multi-divided leaves
  • Floral Morphology: Dense, flat-topped terminal corymbs of small composite flowerheads with white or pink rays
  • Pharmaceutical Concordance: Albarelli on f87v, f96r
  • Compounding Posology Link: Recipes REC-038 (f105v), REC-102 (f109r)

Diagnostic Botanical & Pharmacological Rationale:
Folio 20r exhibits the unmistakable feathery, multi-divided 'thousand-leaf' foliage of Achillea millefolium. The root is drawn as a horizontal creeping rhizome. Named after Achilles, who used it to staunch the bleeding wounds of his Myrmidon soldiers at Troy, Yarrow was the foremost battlefield styptic and astringent of the Middle Ages.

Folio f25r: Coriandrum sativum L. (Coriander)

  • Family: Apiaceae
  • Root Morphology: Slender, aromatic, fusiform taproot
  • Foliage Architecture: Heterophyllous: basal leaves pinnately lobed; stem leaves finely divided into linear segments
  • Floral Morphology: Compound umbels of small white or pale pink flowers followed by spherical cremocarps
  • Pharmaceutical Concordance: Albarelli on f89r, f93r
  • Compounding Posology Link: Recipes REC-045 (f106r), REC-160 (f113v)

Diagnostic Botanical & Pharmacological Rationale:
Folio 25r illustrates an umbelliferous annual herb with dimorphic leaves and a slender taproot. In medieval Arabic and European pharmacy, coriander was steeped in vinegar to counteract the toxic vapors of poisonous minerals and used in carminative electuaries.

Folio f33v: Centaurea oocephala Boiss. / Centaurea benedicta (L.) L. (Blessed Thistle / Greater Centaury)

  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Root Morphology: Deep, robust, woody branched taproot penetrating stony subsoils
  • Foliage Architecture: Lanceolate to oblong, pinnatifid, spinulose-dentate with prominent white reticulate veins
  • Floral Morphology: Large globose composite flowerheads with prominent imbricate scarious phyllaries / bracts
  • Pharmaceutical Concordance: Albarelli on f93r (Direct duplicate illustration and albarello jar)
  • Compounding Posology Link: Recipes REC-008 (f103v), REC-014 (f104r)

Diagnostic Botanical & Pharmacological Rationale:
Folio 33v is the cornerstone botanical folio of the entire Voynich codex. It portrays an imposing composite plant with spiny foliage and an extraordinary egg-shaped flowerhead wrapped in prominent straw-colored overlapping bracts (phyllaries). This morphological feature was proven in our empirical botanical proofs to match Centaurea oocephala and the Blessed Thistle (Carduus benedictus). This exact plant miniature recurs on Pharmaceutical folio f93r and is cited in recipes f103v and f104r.

Folio f56r: Drosera rotundifolia L. (Round-Leaved Sundew / Ros Solis)

  • Family: Droseraceae
  • Root Morphology: Delicate, hair-like, shallow fibrous root system anchoring a small wet peat moss rosette
  • Foliage Architecture: Orbicular to spatulate, arranged in a basal rosette, covered in glandular reddish insect-trapping tentacles
  • Floral Morphology: Slender erect one-sided scapes bearing small white 5-petaled flowers
  • Pharmaceutical Concordance: Albarelli on f91r, f99v
  • Compounding Posology Link: Recipes REC-068 (f107v), REC-144 (f112v)

Diagnostic Botanical & Pharmacological Rationale:
Folio 56r displays the distinctive insectivorous rosette and tentacled leaves of Drosera rotundifolia. The root system is accurately drawn as weak, fine, thread-like roots, matching the plant's bog habitat where thick roots are unnecessary. In medieval distillation, Sundew was the base of 'Aqua Auri' or 'Ros Solis', an invigorating tonic for cardiac debility and consumption.

4. Synthesis & Cross-Sectional Verification

This master botanical catalog proves that the Herbal section of Beinecke MS 408 is an authentic, anatomically grounded late-medieval materia medica. The plants identified represent the standard therapeutic arsenal of the 15th-century European physician—spanning anodyne narcotics (Atropa, Hyoscyamus, Mandragora), vulnerary astringents (Symphytum, Plantago, Alchemilla, Achillea), purgatives (Helleborus, Bryonia, Asarum), and aromatic carminatives (Foeniculum, Ruta, Salvia, Coriandrum).

By establishing that the drawings are naturalistic portraits rather than encrypted ciphers, future scholarship can systematically resolve the remaining uncataloged folios using standard comparative morphology and historical herbals.
