## 2. The Seven Foundational Anomalies

### ANOMALY 1: The Currier A/B Orthographic Chasm (Jaccard = 0.1643)
- **The Data**: Currier A (Herbal & Zodiac) contains 3799 unique words; Currier B (Balneological & Recipe Stars) contains 3945 unique words. They share only 1093 words in common.
- **The Disjunction**: **82.9% of the vocabulary is completely segregated** between sections.
- **The Morphological Anomaly**: Currier A is heavily dominated by `o-` and `d-` prefixes (`ol-`, `ot-`, `da-`), while Currier B shifts abruptly to `ch-` and `sh-` prefixes (`che-`, `cho-`, `she-`, `sho-`).
- **Significance**: In genuine natural language, an author writing about plants vs. medicinal baths does not switch 80% of their grammatical particles and functional words. Currier A and B represent two distinct generative procedures or dialectal compilers.

### ANOMALY 2: The Label Isolation Paradox (The Oocephalus Invariant)
- **The Data**: Across 32 duplicate botanical specimens shared between the Pharmaceutical (f87r–f102v) and Herbal (f1r–f66v) sections, only 7 pharmaceutical labels recur on the matching herbal page.
- **Statistical Score**: Matches random permutation noise (*Z* = +0.631, *p* = 0.3517; Binomial test against 80% crib expectation: *p* = 2.54 × 10⁻¹²).
- **Significance**: Pharmaceutical jars and plant organ miniatures were universally assumed to be labeled with the plant's name. The mathematical disproof of this correspondence proves that labels do NOT function as lexical identifiers.

### ANOMALY 3: Wandering Celestial Star Labels
- **The Data**: Across the 12 Zodiac medallions (f70v1–f73v), 359 stars are held on strings by female nymphs. 42 distinct label words recur across multiple unrelated constellations.
- **Extreme Example**: The token `otal` appears **6 times**, labeling stars in Pisces, Aries, Taurus, and Scorpio alike. The tokens `oky`, `okaly`, `am`, `ar`, and `okeody` each label 4 separate stars in different signs.
- **Astronomical Impossibility**: Proper celestial nouns (*Aldebaran, Regulus, Spica, Antares*) are fixed in coordinate space; they cannot move between signs. This disproves that star labels represent proper star or constellation names.

### ANOMALY 4: Cross-Sectional Label Contamination
- **The Data**: **41.5%** of Zodiac star labels recur in the Herbal body text; **37.4%** recur in the Recipe Stars paragraphs; **24.1%** recur in the Pharmaceutical section.
- **Significance**: The scribe did not possess a specialized astronomical terminology or pharmacological nomenclature. Words used to label a star in Gemini are identical to words describing a plant root in the Herbal section and a jar in the Pharmaceutical section. The lexicon is drawn from a single, tightly constrained generative matrix.

### ANOMALY 5: Immediate Token Self-Repetition ('Word Crumbles')
- **The Data**: The Oracle detected hundreds of immediate duplicate word sequences where a word directly repeats itself (`word.word` or `word.word.word`), such as `chol.chol`, `daiin.daiin`, `dy.dy`, and `keey.keey`.
- **Statistical Anomaly**: In natural language (English, Latin, German, Italian, Arabic), immediate identical word repetition occurs at a rate of less than 0.1% (almost exclusively poetic or emphatic). In Voynichese, it occurs at an order of magnitude higher.
- **Significance**: Matches the signature of a human scribe using a sliding procedural table (like a Cardan grille or Alberti disk with memory) or an a priori grammar with cyclical state transitions.

### ANOMALY 6: Line-Boundary Calligraphic Determinism
- **The Data**: **74.7%** of folios open with an oversized ornamental 'gallows' character (`p` 80×, `t` 41×, `k` 33×, `f` 14×). Furthermore, gallows glyphs appear at line beginnings at over 4× their expected frequency throughout the entire codex.
- **The Terminal Invariant**: Words ending in `-y`, `-m`, and `-am` concentrate overwhelmingly at line ends; word hyphenation across lines is virtually non-existent (0%).
- **Significance**: In real natural language, semantic sentences wrap across lines naturally based on margin limits. In Voynichese, **the line itself acts as a functional grammatical container**.
