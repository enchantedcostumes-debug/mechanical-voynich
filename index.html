📖 The 5 Physical 15th-Century Navigation Finding Aids

Before modern page numbers existed, medieval scribes and physicians relied on sophisticated ergonomic, tactile, and iconographic finding aids built directly into the parchment. Here is how a 15th-century reader navigated MS 408:

1. The Rosettes Map (f85r–f86v)

A monumental 6-panel foldout (45 × 45 cm) featuring 9 interconnected bastions. Functions as the Master Cosmological Table of Contents, linking the heavens, balneological waters, and medicinal apothecary towers.

2. Quire Collation & Edge Tabs

MS 408 contains 16 quires. Large folding folios in Quires 9, 10, and 14 create uneven physical block edges. In the hands, the book block naturally pops open to the astronomical and rosettes sections without flipping page by page.

3. Gallows Line-Initials (74.7%)

74.7% of all folios begin with oversized tall characters (p, t, k, f). These served the exact medieval function of rubricated paragraph index tabs, visible when thumbing through the outer margins.

4. Albarelli Jar Silhouettes

In the Pharmaceutical section (f87r–f102v), distinct line drawings of ceramic drug jars line the outer margins. Readers scanned jar shapes and 1-word labels directly rather than reading running text.

5. Contemporary Latin Month Headers

A 15th-century user inscribed Latin month names (marz, abril, mayo, etc.) into the central medallions of the Zodiac folios, creating explicit seasonal navigation markers.
