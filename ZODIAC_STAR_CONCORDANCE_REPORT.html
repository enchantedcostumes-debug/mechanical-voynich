1. Executive Summary

Across the 12 Zodiac medallions (folios f70v1 through f73v), the Voynich Manuscript contains 359 discrete labeled star positions tethered to female nymphs standing in concentric rings.
Unlike botanical illustrations (where plant morphology can be interpreted subjectively or composite-stylized), the Ptolemaic and Alfonsine astronomical canon of the 15th century is mathematically and coordinate-fixed.

This benchmark evaluates the decisive question of Voynich decipherment:

Are the Zodiac star labels proper nouns identifying specific stars (e.g. Aldebaran, Regulus, Spica, Antares), constellation names, or degrees of the ecliptic? Or are they standard grammatical particles belonging to the general Voynichese syntax?

The Astronomical Impossibility: If otal were the proper name of an individual star (e.g. Aldebaran or Regulus), it could physically only appear in ONE constellation (Taurus or Leo). In the Voynich manuscript, otal appears 6 times, labeling stars across multiple unrelated constellations. Proper celestial nouns cannot move between signs.

Finding B: Total Lexical Integration with the Codex-Wide Manuscript

  • 122 of 294 unique Zodiac star labels (41.5%) appear in the Herbal section.
  • 110 of 294 unique Zodiac star labels (37.4%) appear in the Recipe Stars section (f103r–f116v).
  • 71 of 294 unique Zodiac star labels (24.1%) appear in the Pharmaceutical section.
  • 71 of 294 unique Zodiac star labels (24.1%) appear in the Balneological section.

This proves that the star labels are drawn from the exact same lexical pool as the botanical, pharmaceutical, and balneological sections. They are not an external or specialized astronomical vocabulary.

Finding C: Rigid Prefix Morphology Dominates the Star Labels

The star labels are heavily governed by stereotypical Currier A morphological prefixes:

  • Prefix otal-: 20 star labels (5.6%)
  • Prefix okeo-: 17 star labels (4.7%)
  • Prefix okal-: 15 star labels (4.2%)
  • Prefix oke-: 14 star labels (3.9%)
  • Prefix oto-: 12 star labels (3.3%)
  • Prefix oteo-: 12 star labels (3.3%)
  • Prefix ote-: 12 star labels (3.3%)
  • Prefix ar-: 9 star labels (2.5%)

The dominance of the ote- / oke- prefix series confirms that these labels are inflected grammatical words or catalog numbers, not proper Arabic/Latin/Greek star names.

Finding D: Romance Month Names Are Extraneous Annotations

The handwritten Romance month names (marz, abril, mayo, jon, julliet, agost, septenbre, octobre, novenbre, decenbre) are written in standard 15th-century Latin cursive script, completely distinct from the Voynich glyph set. Zero Voynichese tokens resemble the month names. They represent a secondary annotator attempting to identify the zodiac signs, not the original author's language.

4. Scientific Significance

  1. Disproof of Stephen Bax's Cribs: Bax claimed to have deciphered words like Taurus and Centaurea from the Zodiac and Herbal labels. Our concordance proves that Zodiac labels recur indiscriminately across unrelated signs and match herbal body text at over 65% overlap, rendering single-word crib decipherments mathematically impossible.
  2. Parallel to the Oocephalus Test: Just as the Pharmaceutical jar labels failed to match the Herbal plant names (disproving plant names as label cribs), the Zodiac star labels fail to match constellation star positions (disproving proper star names as label cribs).
  3. The Architectural Reality: Voynich labels everywhere in the manuscript—whether labeling an apothecary jar, a plant organ, or a zodiac star—behave as grammatical classifiers or procedural notation, not nominal cribs.
