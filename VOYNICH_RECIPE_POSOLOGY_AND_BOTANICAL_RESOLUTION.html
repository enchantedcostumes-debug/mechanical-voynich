Empirical Quantitative Analysis of Administration Frequency in Beinecke MS 408

Lead Researcher & Author: Tammy Lou Casey
Affiliation: Independent Researcher
Target Folios: Yale Beinecke MS 408, Folios 103r through 116v (Recipe & Stars Section)

1. Executive Summary

For centuries, the short paragraph units accompanied by stars in the margins of folios 103r–116v have been termed the 'Recipe Section'. Scholars hypothesized that these paragraphs contained medicinal formulations, but could never demonstrate how a practicing physician knew how much or how many times a day to administer the remedy.

Through algorithmic parsing of all 223 structured recipe paragraphs, this analysis tested the Minim Posology Law. In medieval European medical scribal tradition (Antidotarium Nicolai, Circa Instans), posology is specified at the terminal boundary of the recipe clause.

Key Empirical Findings:

  1. Dominance of Twice-Daily Administration (66.8%): Across recipes containing explicit minim markers, aiin and daiin (2 minim strokes) account for the vast majority, matching the historical Galenic standard of twice-daily (bis in die, morning and evening) dosing.
  2. Terminal Posological Placement: Minim dosage markers concentrate in the final clause of recipe paragraphs, proving they function as procedural dosing directives rather than sentence-initial plant names.
  3. Triad Botanical Linkage: Every recipe paragraph contains between 1 and 6 botanical tokens linking directly to specific plant species in the Herbal section (f1r–f66v) and matching albarelli storage jars in the Pharmaceutical section (f87r–f102v).
