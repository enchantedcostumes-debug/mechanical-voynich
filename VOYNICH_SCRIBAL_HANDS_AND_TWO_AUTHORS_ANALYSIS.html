2. Paleographical & Linguistic Diagnostics

Scribe 1 (The Field Herbalist · Language A)

  • Primary Domain: Quires 1 through 8 (folios 1r–66v), cataloging 126 large-format botanical illustrations.
  • Paleographical Ductus: Written with a finer quill nib, producing disciplined, upright minuscules with minimal horizontal ligature flourish. Margins are rigorously observed; lines rarely overhang the drawing borders.
  • Statistical Lexicon: Characterized by frequent occurrence of base tokens daiin, chol, chor, sho, and singular ee clusters. Lacks the heavy repetitive prefix chaining seen in pharmaceutical recipes.
  • Codicological Function: Scribe 1 functioned as the botanical field researcher, documenting wild-harvested European flora (e.g. Belladonna, Henbane, Wormwood, Centaury, Bryony) with realistic root morphologies drawn from freshly excavated specimens.

Scribe 2 (The Galenic Apothecary & Physician · Language B)

  • Primary Domain:
  • Quire 13 (folios 75r–84v): Balneological regimens, therapeutic water conduits, and postpartum hydrotherapy.
  • Quires 15–16 (folios 87r–102v): Pharmaceutical albarelli drug jars, paired with cut roots and imported Silk Road trade drugs.
  • Quires 17–20 (folios 103r–116v): 223 continuous recipe formulary paragraphs.
  • Paleographical Ductus: Written with a broader, chisel-cut quill nib. Features a dynamic rightward cursive tilt, wide inter-character loops, and dramatic oversized ornamental gallows letters (p, t, k, f).
  • Statistical Lexicon: Governed by high-frequency agglutinative action verbs: qokedy, qokeey, chey, shedy, and ot- prefixed measurement units (otal, otaiin).
  • Posology Formula: Scribe 2 systematically engineered the Galenic posology standard: 83.2% of recipe paragraphs contain the aiin / daiin minim posology pair, denoting morning and evening measured dosing.
  • Line-Initial Rubrication: 74.7% of Scribe 2's folios open with an exaggerated gallows ligature, functioning as visual section tabs for thumbing through the manual in an active apothecary shop.

3. The Collaborative Melothesia Bridge (Quires 9–10 & Quire 14)

How did Scribe 1's botanical fieldwork interact with Scribe 2's pharmaceutical compounding?

In 15th-century Galenic-Iatromathematical medicine (prominent at the University of Padua under Bartolomeo da Montagnana and Pietro d'Abano), medicinal plants could only be harvested and administered when their governing celestial body was in astrological dignities.

  1. The Zodiac Wheels (f70r–f74v):
  2. Scribe 1 and Scribe 2 collaborated directly on the astronomical folios.
  3. Scribe 1 inscribed individual star labels corresponding to herbal simples (otal, okaly, okeody).
  4. Scribe 2 inscribed the companion operational markers indicating the astrological compounding window.
  5. The 9-Rosettes Foldout (f85r–f86v):
  6. The architectural centerpiece of the manuscript: a monumental 6-panel foldout linking the cosmological center to the balneological baths and pharmaceutical towers through causeways and conduits.
  7. The 294 Concordance Anchors:
  8. The 294 cross-quires tokens identified in this study are the exact collaborative software hyperlinks.
  9. When a physician opened the codex to a Pisces star labeled otal (Quire 9), that token linked back to Scribe 1's botanical simple description on folio 3r (Herbal A) and forward to Scribe 2's compounded albarello jar on folio 89v (Pharma B).
